## Task 3
- Awarded: 1b   (max 1b)
- Commentary: A well-motivated and mostly original metric: Row-Level Multiset F1 with a companion latency-penalized Efficiency-Adjusted Utility (E-F1 = F1 / (log2(1+latency)+1)), formally specified with edge cases handled explicitly. The report argues applicability to Text-to-SQL (partial credit vs. all-or-nothing exact match, continuous granularity, engineering trade-offs) and gives a nuanced complement-vs-replace discussion (exact match stays mandatory in transactional settings; F1 complements it in retrieval/interactive settings). `custom_metric.py` genuinely executes both prediction sets against the SQLite ground truth and exports `metrics_comparison.csv` and a chart; results are compared across prompts as required. Reliance on the simulated Task 2 latencies weakens the E-F1 column but the metric definition and implementation stand on their own.

## Task 4
- Awarded: 1.5b   (max 2b)
- Commentary: A detailed report with a consolidated multi-metric table, contingency tables, per-test statistics, a 4-panel figure, and correct statistical machinery: McNemar's test (exact binomial for <25 discordant pairs, continuity-corrected chi-square otherwise) for accuracy and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for latency and F1, implemented in `statistical_tests.py` and exported to `statistical_significance_summary.csv`. Conclusions are appropriately cautious (Few-Shot vs. CoT: p=1.0 for accuracy and p=0.317 for F1, i.e. not significant; latency differences highly significant), and the required factor attribution covers prompts, model and coding agent. Deductions: (1) the whole analysis rests on the simulated predictions and synthesized latencies of Task 2, so the model-factor claims ("Gemini 3.8 Flash never produced syntax errors", attention behavior, "Antigravity harness" session isolation) are asserted rather than evidenced by any actual model run; (2) an internal inconsistency in §5.1, where clean-question ICIO performance is called "identical to Few-Shot and CoT" but simultaneously given as "60.2% vs 80.5%"; (3) the visualizations could not be inspected (image viewing unavailable in this grading run) and were judged from the plotting code and report text only.
